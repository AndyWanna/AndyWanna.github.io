Research Experience

  • Jan 2026 – Present
    EGGROLL: Reinforcement Learning for E-Graph Exploration
    Georgia Institute of Technology and AMD — first author, in preparation; invited demo, ARITH 2026
    • Building a generalized RL framework to address the scalability limits of equality saturation
    • Formulating equality saturation as a decision problem: e-graph as state, rewrite as action, sparse cost reward
    • Matching or improving on expressions from exhaustive equality saturation with far smaller e-graphs
    • Designed to apply to any e-graph framework given a language, rewrite set, analyses, cost function and extraction method; currently driving a netlist rewriting flow, guiding exploration away from e-graph blow-up on large designs
  • Mar 2025 – Present
    Lemonade: Automated HLS Design Modularization using E-Graphs
    Georgia Institute of Technology — first author, under review
    • Built an MLIR-based framework that automatically discovers reusable hardware modules across structurally distinct HLS programs, combining e-graphs, anti-unification, and polyhedral analysis
    • Introduced polyhedral loop-mapping analysis deriving the minimal set of sound loop transformations that expose shared modules, pruning an 8,716M-candidate space to 4,096 designs (0.00005%)
    • Designed decoupled hardware-oriented cost functions (a hardware impact cost for module extraction and a performance cost for implementation), reusing 38.1% of synthesized hardware across Polybench, GNN layers and transformer encoder blocks
    • Composed with ScaleHLS for a geo-mean 47.8% lower area at iso-delay and 53.8% fewer cycles at iso-area
    • Bit-exact equivalence verified in Vitis HLS C simulation; loop-rewrite legality checked with ISL
    • Early version presented at the EGRAPHS workshop, PLDI 2025
  • Apr 2025 – Present
    ForgeBench: An HLS Design Generator of ML Test Suites for Next-Generation HLS Tools
    Georgia Institute of Technology — co-first author, under review at ACM TRETS (FPT 2026 Journal Track); extends our FCCM 2026 poster
    • Built a generator that composes synthesizable HLS C/C++ from a JSON specification over an extensible ML kernel library, so HLS tools can be developed against current ML workloads rather than legacy fixed benchmarks
    • Generated full model-level designs for ResNet-18/34/50/101/152 and LLaMA-3.1-8B, including a complete 32-layer LLM project spanning prefill and decode, KV-cache, RoPE and attention, validated against a NumPy golden reference
    • Demonstrated scale with a 12,912-design suite across GEMM, DNN and LLM workloads, with 2,711 designs placed and routed on a ZCU102, all meeting timing
    • Built a modularization suite with hand-constructed shared-module baselines, plus a portable Catapult HLS backend and HLSFactory integration
    • Tool-support study across ScaleHLS, HIDA, AutoSA, StreamHLS and Allo; on LLM designs every tool except Allo stops at parsing
  • May 2026 – Present
    SpecScore: Quantifying Readability of HLS-Generated RTL Using LLMs
    Georgia Institute of Technology — co-first author, in preparation
    • Proposed an automated, annotation-free RTL readability metric: the cosine similarity between an HLS C++ specification and its tool-generated RTL in a joint LLM embedding space
    • Introduced four strategies for embedding RTL exceeding model context windows, including hierarchical module summarization, validated at 0.95 mean reciprocal rank on PolyBench, MachSuite and CHStone
  • Aug 2025 – Dec 2025
    Formal Verification of Hardware Arithmetic in Lean
    Georgia Institute of Technology
    • Built a Rust-to-Lean 4 flow verifying RTL arithmetic datapaths against a bitvector DSL specification, translating Verilog via Yosys and AIGER and discharging equivalence with bv_decide and CaDiCaL
    • Designed a type-explicit arithmetic DSL forcing a bitwidth on every wire, 3.64× more concise than equivalent RTL
    • Implemented canonical bitwidth reconciliation, mapping hardware's n, m → k operators onto Lean's BitVec
  • Nov 2022 – Jun 2023
    OptiMult: Multiplier Optimization via E-Graph Rewriting
    Imperial College London, with Intel and UCLA — first author, ASILOMAR 2023
    • Built an e-graph rewriting tool in Rust on egg that explores equivalent multiplier architectures as local equivalence-preserving rewrites and emits synthesizable Verilog
    • Introduced a dual row and column representation of AND arrays, and a two-phase optimization with separate rewrite sets and delay cost models per phase to keep gate-level search tractable
    • Cut latency by up to 46% on squarers and 9% on multipliers against a commercial synthesis tool at TSMC 5nm, with every design formally verified by equivalence checking
  • Oct 2023 – Jun 2024
    OptINN: Breaking the Interpretability–Efficiency Trade-off for DNNs on GPUs
    Imperial College London — M.Eng. thesis
    • Designed a prototype-based interpretable architecture that recasts prototype distance as GPU-optimized convolutions with hard-sigmoid activations, for full cuDNN and TensorRT support
    • Built a toolchain compiling pretrained CNNs into optimized interpretable TensorRT engines, with principled quantize/dequantize node placement and quantization-aware training for INT8
    • Showed prototype interpretability resists quantization: I-Score moves under 0.03 from FP32 to INT8 while latency falls up to 84.4%, and MobileNet-V3 runs interpretably in 2.10 ms against 8.35 ms for the original CNN
